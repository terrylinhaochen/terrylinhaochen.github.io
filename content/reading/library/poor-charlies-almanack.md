## One discipline is not enough

This collection of Munger's talks draws across business, psychology, history, science, and ethics. Its organizing idea is to assemble multiple mental models rather than force every decision through the method you know best. Different explanations can reveal different failure modes in the same situation.

## Use models to challenge a decision

For a live choice, describe the incentives, the base rates, and the ways your preferred outcome could fail. Ask whether each model changes a prediction or exposes a hidden assumption. This exercise is an application of the multidisciplinary approach; merely naming more concepts does not make the analysis stronger.

## Keep competence and outcome separate

A successful person's account is useful evidence, but not proof that every habit caused the success. Distinguish a durable reasoning method from a particular historical investment. Keep uncertainty visible and seek disconfirming information. The book belongs in a decision-making toolkit, not in place of independent research or an assessment of your own financial constraints.
