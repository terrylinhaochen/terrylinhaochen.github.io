## Technology development is a political choice

Karp and Zamiska argue for a closer relationship between the technology industry, national purpose, and defense. Read this as a normative argument about what technological capability should serve, not a neutral account of where innovation naturally goes.

## Ask who defines the mission

For each proposed public purpose, identify who chooses it, who gains power, and who is accountable for failure or misuse. A shared mission can mobilize resources while still leaving serious disagreements about rights, priorities, and the use of force. The existence of technical capacity does not settle those disagreements.

## Keep the authors' position visible

The authors write from within the technology and national-security world they discuss. That perspective provides experience and also warrants scrutiny. Compare their diagnosis with alternative views of public-interest technology, democratic oversight, and civilian needs. The useful reading outcome is a clearer account of the institutional choices involved, not automatic agreement with the proposed program.
