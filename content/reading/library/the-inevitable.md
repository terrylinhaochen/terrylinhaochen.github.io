## Look for a direction, not a product prediction

Kelly organizes technological change around recurring processes such as accessing, sharing, filtering, and remixing. The useful claim is that individual inventions sit inside longer changes in how information and services move. A failed product does not necessarily disprove a larger shift, and a successful launch does not prove its timing was inevitable.

## Separate capability from adoption

A practical reading exercise is to choose one process and trace the constraints around it. What has become cheaper? What still requires trust, coordination, permission, or behavior change? This is an application of Kelly's framework, not a forecast that a particular company or technology must win.

## Keep the thesis open to correction

The book is deliberately optimistic. Treat its confident language as a hypothesis to examine, not a deadline to accept. Write down an observable sign that would support your chosen trend and another that would weaken it. The goal is better questions about technological change, not certainty about the future.
