## Moving others is ordinary work

Pink broadens selling beyond a sales job to the everyday effort of persuading people to devote attention, effort, or resources. He argues that better-informed buyers change the relationship: influence cannot rely as easily on one side possessing all the relevant information.

## Start with perspective and clarity

The book's central qualities are attunement, buoyancy, and clarity: understanding another perspective, handling rejection, and helping people see a problem more clearly. Before polishing a pitch, ask whether you have identified the right problem and understood the person's context.

## Make service the test

A practical exercise is to rewrite a request around the benefit to the person receiving it, then make the next action specific and optional. If the only benefit belongs to you, improve the offer rather than the pressure. Read the persuasion techniques through this ethical boundary: influence should leave people better informed and able to choose, not merely more likely to comply.
