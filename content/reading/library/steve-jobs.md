## Read a life, not a management template

Isaacson's biography follows Jobs through product creation, company building, and personal relationships. It offers a setting for examining the connection between an individual's taste and the organizations needed to turn that taste into products. Keep the consequences for collaborators in the picture.

## Distinguish conviction from its costs

Choose a decision in the narrative and separate the intended product benefit from the interpersonal or organizational cost of pursuing it. Ask whether that cost was necessary, merely tolerated, or obscured by success. This is a critical reading method, not a claim that intensity itself explains innovation.

## Look beyond the central character

A biography naturally concentrates attention on its subject. Reconstruct the contributions of teams, partners, technical foundations, and timing before concluding that an outcome came from one personality. The most useful application is to clarify a standard of product quality you can communicate and test, without treating another person's harmful behavior as a requirement for excellence.
