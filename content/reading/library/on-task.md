## Knowing a goal is not executing it

Badre introduces cognitive control: how the brain organizes behavior to achieve goals. The distinction matters because having the relevant knowledge does not by itself explain how a person selects the right action at the right moment. Context, rules, and competing demands shape that translation.

## Notice the structure of a task

For one difficult activity, distinguish the overall goal from the immediate action and the cues that connect them. Where do you have to remember a rule, switch between contexts, or decide which information matters? This is a practical observation exercise, not a neurological diagnosis.

## Avoid the single-switch explanation

A neuroscience account is not a promise that one productivity trick will unlock the brain. Read explanations at their intended level and distinguish laboratory findings from broad workplace advice. A modest application is to make task rules and next actions easier to retrieve, then observe whether the change helps in the situation you actually face.
